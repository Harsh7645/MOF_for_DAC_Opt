1. Scope of Research & Novelty 
This research proposes leveraging a Spiking Neural Network (SNN) based Quadratic 
Programming (QP) framework to optimize the structural design of Metal-Organic Frameworks 
(MOFs) for Direct Air Capture (DAC) of CO2. The primary bottleneck in reticular chemistry is 
the "combinatorial explosion" of potential metal nodes and organic linkers, rendering traditional 
screening methods computationally intractable for large-scale discovery. 
What we are claiming to do: Given pairwise interaction parameters ({h_i, J_ij}) sourced from 
DFT databases and heuristics, we aim to find the MOF building-block combination that 
minimizes the total Hamiltonian. We will then verify if our SNN-QP derived configurations match 
known, high-performing MOFs from databases like CoRE.  
Novelty: While traditional High-Throughput Screening evaluates materials iteratively, our 
approach maps the combinatorial design space into a continuous neuromorphic architecture. 
By utilizing continuous relaxation on the SNN-QP solver and comparing it against recent 
quantum-annealing material discovery methods, we aim to offer a highly parallel, 
energy-efficient paradigm for isolating low-energy structural configurations.  
2. Target Material Class 
We will target Amine-functionalized Zirconium-based MOFs (e.g., UiO-66 derivatives). 
Zirconium-based MOFs are renowned for their exceptional hydrothermal stability, which is a 
strict prerequisite for DAC applications where moisture is prevalent. By focusing on 
amine-functionalized linkers, we introduce chemisorption sites that selectively bind CO2 at 
ultra-low concentrations (400 ppm), while the optimization framework will tune the steric

3. Core Formulation & QUBO Mapping 
To interface with the SNN-QP framework, the chemical configuration must be mapped to a 
Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problem. The objective is to minimize 
the pseudo-energy Hamiltonian (H) of the system. 
Decision Variables & Continuous Relaxation  
While the strict QUBO formulation treats building block selection as a discrete binary choice, 
our SNN-QP framework operates continuously. Therefore, we apply continuous relaxation, 
defining the variable as a fractional inclusion: x_i in [0, 1]. 
The variable x_i represents the continuous state of a specific building block (e.g., an 
amine-tagged BDC linker or a Zirconium cluster node). The lab's SNN-QP solver will process 
these continuous variables to find the optimal approximate state. Post-optimization, we will 
threshold the continuous output back to the discrete domain {0, 1} to yield the final physical 
MOF configuration. 
Objective Function 
Minimize H(x) = ∑(i) h_i * x_i + ∑(i < j) J_ij * x_i * x_j 
Parameter Definitions 
●​ Linear Bias (h_i): Represents the intrinsic CO2 binding affinity (adsorption enthalpy) of 
the individual component i. Favorable CO2 binding translates to a negative (low energy) 
h_i penalty. 
●​ Quadratic Interaction (J_ij): Represents the structural and geometric compatibility 
between component i and component j. 
○​ Steric Clash: If the two components cannot physically co-exist due to size 
constraints, J_ij is assigned a massive positive penalty. 
○​ Synergistic Pore Formation: If the combination yields a pore size precisely tuned to 
the kinetic diameter of CO2 (approx. 3.3 Å), J_ij acts as an energetic reward 
(negative value). 
4. Baseline Comparison Methods

To validate the efficacy and speed of the SNN-QP solver, our results will be benchmarked 
against the following classical baselines: 
●​ Simulated Annealing (SA): A standard heuristic classical algorithm for solving QUBO 
formulations, used to compare Time-to-Solution (TTS). 
●​ Gurobi Optimizer: An industry-standard mathematical programming solver for 
quadratic optimization, providing exact solutions for smaller combinatorial subsets to 
verify the SNN's accuracy. 
●​ Surrogate-Model-Driven Screening: We will use Graph Neural Network (GNN) 
potentials (such as MOFTransformer or CGCNN) as our primary competitive baseline. 
These machine learning models rank candidate structures rapidly, after which the top-K 
candidates are validated by DFT. We will benchmark our SNN-QP optimization speed and 
accuracy against these GNN ranking methods.
